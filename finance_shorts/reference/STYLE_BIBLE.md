# Style Bible — derived from the reference short

Source: the reference video shared by the channel owner (Google Drive). 61.07 s · 720×1280 (9:16) · 30 fps · H.264 + AAC 44.1 kHz stereo · integrated loudness **−14.1 LUFS**.
Analysed at 4 frames/s (all 244 frames reviewed) plus full-resolution stills and audio measurements.
The video is kept out of git (copyright). Re-download it with `tools/fetch_reference.sh`.

---

## 1. What it is in one line

**Flat 2D cartoon characters (thick outlines, white round heads, stick limbs) acting inside richly lit, stylised-realistic 3D worlds,** cut fast to a
single expressive narrator. Captions show one or two words at a time. The story is true, strange and historical, and it ends on an ironic punchline.

## 2. Story structure (the part that matters most)

| Beat | Time | Reference | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open: absurd action, no context | 0–3.5 s | "He loaded a shotgun with gold and fired it into the walls of the mine." | Gives the viewer an image that makes no sense, so they want it explained. |
| Voice of the viewer | 3.5–9 s | Buddy: "So we're shooting gold… into a gold mine?" | A side character asks the question the audience is thinking. |
| The reveal and the term | 9–10.6 s | "You see, it's called **salting**." | Names the trick, so the viewer learns something. |
| Backstory, fast | 10.7–16 s | Dug 10 years, found nothing. Newspaper: "MILLIONAIRE DUE TOMORROW". | Stakes and motive in two shots. |
| Escalation and detail | 16–34.6 s | Last $100 on gold dust, rich buyer arrives, the expert tests and finds flattened shot. | Tension: will they get caught? |
| Twist #1 | 34.7–43 s | Rich guy laughs: "Why would anyone shoot gold into a wall? Insane." Duo nods: "Completely insane." | Dramatic irony and comedy. |
| Payoff | 43–46 s | Rich guy pays up. They run with the cash. | First resolution. |
| Twist #2 and lesson | 46–61 s | Rich guy blasts the tunnel with dynamite and finds a real vein **10 ft past where the last guy gave up**. | The irony hits. The video ends hard, no outro, so it loops. |

**Rules taken from this:** (1) the first sentence is a visual paradox; (2) a side character voices the viewer's question; (3) teach one
named concept; (4) two twists; (5) the last line reframes the whole story and acts as the lesson; (6) no intro, no "subscribe", hard
ending → loop.

## 3. Pacing

* **~36 shots in 61 s, average shot ≈ 1.7 s.** The longest hold is ~4.5 s, and only on a character delivering a comedic line.
* The narration never pauses for more than about 0.3 s. Pictures change on stressed words ("**fired**", "**gold**", "**salting**", "**dynamite**").
* There's a cut or a big camera move about every 1–2 s. Macro inserts (gun breech, gold dust, gold pan, knife-tip flake) reset attention every 8–10 s.

## 4. Characters

* **Construction:** a big pure-white circle head (about 40% of body height), stick limbs drawn as a single thick dark line with rounded caps, ball hands,
  and a pill or oval torso dressed in flat colour shapes (plaid, buttons, rose, suspenders). Outline is a dark chocolate/black stroke, about 6–8 px at 720 w,
  same weight on every character.
* **Faces:** two dot eyes, with brows doing most of the acting. Mouth swaps between closed line, grin with teeth, open "O", grimace and smirk.
  Squint-smile (∩ ∩) for smug characters. Hair is just a few strokes.
* **Identity comes from costume:** prospector (wide-brim hat with headlamp, red plaid vest), buddy (gold knit beanie, teal sweater),
  rich guy (white top hat, white suit, curled moustache, red rose), expert (bowler, round glasses, maroon coat).
  Each character gets **one strong signature colour**.
* **Animation:** cut-out/limited style with pose swaps, head tilts, blinks, mouth flaps on stressed syllables, and occasional full-body gags
  (head bonk on beam, leg kick while laughing, dive from explosion). They sometimes look into the lens.
* **Integration into 3D:** characters are flat cards placed in the 3D scene. They're scaled by perspective, take on the scene's light colour,
  **cast soft shadows on the ground**, and sit inside the depth of field (foreground characters blur when the focus pulls).

> For our channel we design an **original** cast in this genre (different silhouettes, costumes and names) and don't copy the reference's characters.

## 5. Environments and props

* Stylised-realistic 3D in the style of a game engine: real-scale geometry with slightly painterly PBR textures (rock, timber, wood floor, dirt).
* **Lighting is the look:** warm amber tungsten (lanterns, candles) in interiors, volumetric light shafts and floating dust motes, strong bloom
  on bright sources. Exteriors use golden-hour daylight with soft haze. Colour palette leans hard into **amber/ochre/gold** with teal accents.
* Hero props are fully 3D and detailed: shotgun, newspaper with readable period typography, $100 bill, gold pan, vintage car, briefcase of cash,
  dynamite. **Readable in-world text** carries story info: "SURE THING MINE · EST. 1899", "THE HOPEFUL HERALD", "CANDLES 40 LBS".
* Hand-drawn chalk annotations on top of the 3D scene for the final reveal ("10 FT ↑" with a measuring tape).

## 6. Camera language

* **The camera is never locked off.** It slowly pushes in on dialogue and tracks with projectiles (gold shot, dynamite).
* Low ground-level angles (over rocks, over rail ties), with the foreground blurred.
* Macro inserts with very shallow depth of field and a slow push. There's a POV shot through a magnifying glass with a ring vignette.
* Whip-pans and match moves between shots. A dust-out to full screen is used as a transition (55.5–56 s).

## 7. Captions

* **1–2 words per caption**, hard cut on each word (no bounce or pop), sentence case with punctuation kept ("salting.", "wall?").
* White, heavy geometric sans (about Montserrat/Poppins ExtraBold–Black), thin dark stroke and soft shadow, no box.
* Horizontally centred and placed **between ~52% and ~68% of frame height**, moved per shot so it never covers a face.
* Cap height is about 4% of frame height.

## 8. Audio

* **Narrator:** a single, very expressive storyteller. Measured pitch spans 134–302 Hz (p10–p90), median ≈195 Hz, which means a big, animated
  delivery (dramatic pauses, comedic emphasis, character voices for dialogue lines). Close-miked and dry, with no room reverb.
* **The bed never drops out** (the quietest 0.1 s window is still around −34 dBFS): there's a constant light score and ambience under the voice.
* **SFX on every action:** gun load and shot, ricochet sparkle, cart rumble, paper flap, cash slap, car engine, head bonk, pick strike, fuse hiss,
  explosion, dust whoosh.
* **Master at −14 LUFS** (YouTube/IG target). The voice sits clearly on top, with the music about 18–22 dB below it.

## 9. Translating this to a finance channel

| Reference element | Our version |
|---|---|
| True, strange gold-rush anecdote | True, strange **money / business** stories (case studies, scams, habits, famous blunders) |
| "Salting" = the named concept | One named finance concept per video (compounding, charm pricing, Ponzi scheme, sunk-cost fallacy…) |
| Mine, saloon, general store | Period-accurate US settings: 1920s Boston office, 1970s garage, 1990s suburban kitchen, Wall Street, small-town Vermont |
| Props carry story text | Cheques, stock tickers, newspapers, contracts, receipts and bank statements with readable numbers |
| Ending irony | The last line reframes the money lesson ("…and the $800 he sold is now worth $300 billion.") |
