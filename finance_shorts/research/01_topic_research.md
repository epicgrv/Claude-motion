# Step 1: Topic research (2026-11-19)

**Selection rule (from the reference):** a *true* money/business story with (a) an absurd opening image, (b) one named concept to teach,
(c) an ironic final twist, and (d) a physical, visual world we can build in 3D. It should be US-centred and fit 45–60 s as a Short.

## What the data says (vidIQ, last ~3 months, English Shorts)

* Story-led business Shorts are breaking out far above their channels' averages. "He Failed at Selling Crepes Until One Secret…" did 1.7M views (33× its channel's average).
  "How $100 Became $204,984: The Egg-Selling Mindset" did 370K (415×). "Why Prices End in 99" did 218K (26×) on a 90-sub channel. "How Cheap Phone Shops Earn
  Money" did 697K (8.3×). "How a College Dropout Built a $31 Billion Empire" did 1M (7×).
* Rising searches this month in the money/business niche: *"how they make billions"* (+1,241%), *"the entire history of coca-cola"* (+262%),
  *"the deadliest candy ever sold"* (+368%), *"retiree math"* (+175%), *"price of risk"*, *"financial engineering"*.
  Viewers are searching for **"the story behind the money"**, not dry tips.
* Pure-advice Shorts ("7 bad habits that keep you poor") get views but very low engagement (0.1%). The stories that outperform are narrative ones,
  which is exactly what the reference's format does.

### My recommendation

1. **Pepsi Jet:** the strongest absurd visual ("a teenager vs. a fighter jet"), a hidden pricing loophole, a courtroom, and a final twist
   (Pepsi changes the ad). It's the closest structural match to the reference: a scheme, a powerful party that laughs, and an ironic ending.
2. **Ronald Read:** the best fit for the *financial habits* pillar. The reveal-at-the-end structure mirrors the "10 ft past" ending perfectly.
3. **Ronald Wayne:** the cleanest ironic punchline for business case studies.

**Fact-check gate:** before scripting, I verify every number and date for the chosen topic against primary or major sources (court
records, newspaper archives, SEC data) and list the sources in the script file. For brand stories (Pepsi, McDonald's, Coca-Cola) we show
**generic, unbranded** cans/logos in the 3D world and only name the companies in narration. That's standard commentary practice and keeps
trademark risk low.
